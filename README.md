# Skii Pronos

Football predictions in French (English on request): every competition ESPN covers loads by itself, every match gets a model pick with an honest hit chance, there's a **coupon du jour** and a **grosse cote du jour** every day plus a big-odds generator, live and full AI analysis on any match, goal alerts in the browser / Telegram / WhatsApp, a public track record, and accounts that sync everything.

## Pages

| Page | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Accueil / Dashboard** | Coupon du jour, grosse cote, live now, top picks for the next 24 h, value spots, your 7-day record |
| **Matchs** | 224 football competitions + NBA / NFL / NHL / MLB + European hockey (SHL, Liiga… via The Odds API), yesterday → +2 days. Filters: live / upcoming / finished, day, sport, men's / women's / youth, league, search |
| **En direct** | Everything in play, scores and minutes refreshed every 15 s |
| **Coupon du jour** | The safest coupon (~2.00, strong legs only) and the grosse cote (x100), AI-checked, plus a builder for any target 1.5 → 10 |
| **Grosse cote** | Generator for 10 → 1000 (or any number): best possible chance at the target, "another one" for new combinations |
| **Match page** | Engine pick + value pick, every market with chance / fair odds / price / EV, live model, stats, key moments, form, table, lineups, bookmaker comparison, **AI analyst**, team strength |
| **Historique** | Every game the engine picked (graded automatically), the daily coupons archive, and your own tracked coupons with stakes and profit |

| **Notifications** | In-site inbox, push alerts (site closed), Telegram / WhatsApp link, followed matches |

## How the prono engine works

1. **Market** — bookmaker odds (DraftKings via ESPN; Pinnacle optional) with the margin removed (power de-vig).
2. **Goals model** — a Dixon-Coles Poisson model fitted to those fair odds prices every market consistently from one score matrix.
3. **Trained strength model** (football) — every club's attack and defence, learnt game by game (see *Football* below), pulled into the market's expected goals by a weight fitted per league tier against the closing line; without a price it stands alone. Hockey and the NFL have their own trained engines. Leagues the trainer never saw fall back to the league table, recent form and **Elo-style team ratings**.
4. **Calibration** — what the engine learns from its own graded picks, and only what a walk-forward test showed to help (`lib/calibration.js` has the figures): a game a bookmaker prices keeps the model's chance untouched; a game with no price shows a chance corrected by its market's learnt offset. Chance-bucket shifts and per-league offsets were removed in November 2026: on 4,527 picks forecast day by day they made the chances worse (log loss 0.5822 against 0.5760 for the model alone), and league labels were anti-predictive (leagues tagged weak went on to beat the model by 6 points). The record page still shows each league's run, as history.
5. **Pick** — the market with the best balance of hit chance and price with a confidence tier. A market with a real price comes first; a market without one (≈ estimated price, shown as the minimum odds worth taking) may only be one of the **standard markets** (1X2, double chance, draw no bet, over/under 1.5 – 3.5, BTTS, team totals 1.5, the two main combos), never below 1.30, and only when it is clearly safer than the priced ones. A refund (draw no bet, whole-number lines) counts as half a win when picking and grading, so "not losing 77 %" never dresses up a 50 % win.
6. **Coupons** — the grosse-cote optimizer keeps, for each match, the leg that gives up the least chance per unit of odds and finishes with the leg that closes the gap for the smallest loss; estimated prices cost 8 % more and fill at most a third of a slip (none in the coupon du jour's first attempts), exotic markets never go in unpriced, and games without bookmaker prices stay out entirely.
7. **Closing line value** — after each game the cron looks up the bookmaker's closing price on the pick's market (ESPN keeps it) and stores how far the pick beat it (`clv` = pick odds / closing odds − 1). The record page shows the average and how often the close was beaten: the long-run signal, independent of a good or bad week.
8. **Live** — remaining goals re-priced from minute, score, red cards and shots.

### Football

The football model (`lib/soccer.js`, maths in `lib/soccer-core.js`, parameters in `lib/soccer-params.json`, report in `scripts/soccer-report.md`) is an **online Dixon-Coles**: each club carries an attack and a defence on a log-goals scale, moved after every game by one Poisson gradient step (fast for a new club, settling after a handful of games, shrunk toward the league mean at each new season); each competition carries its own scoring rate and home advantage, learnt the same way; a slow offset per domestic league, learnt from cup and continental games only, makes a Bundesliga side and a Serbian side comparable. Expected goals are `μ_league · e^{±home/2} · e^{att − def (± offset gap)}`, the score matrix is Poisson × Dixon-Coles ρ. `npm run train:soccer` downloads two seasons and the current one for 73 competitions (every domestic league in the catalogue plus the continental cups and the main domestic cups — `scripts/soccer-history.mjs`, resumable, into `.cache/soccer`) together with the closing line ESPN's core API keeps for finished games (DraftKings this season, ESPN BET before: 1X2, total, spread, opening prices too), fits the dozen global parameters by maximum likelihood of the exact scores walk-forward, fits the market weight per tier on 1X2 log loss against the closing line, scores the whole thing on a season the fit never saw, then refits on everything and ships every club's and competition's current state as the seed `lib/ratings.js` keeps updating (`skii_teams.att/def`, `skii_leagues`); games before the seed's date are not applied twice.

What the hold-out season (2025-26, 18,351 games, 15,841 with a closing line) says — and it matters for how to bet: on every game the model's 1X2 log loss is 1.022 against 1.070 for league frequencies, so it is a real model, and its favourites are if anything under-confident (a 60–70 % favourite lands 71 % of the time, a 70–80 % one 77 %). But on games the bookmaker prices, the **closing line is sharper than the model** (0.997 vs 1.026, in every tier down to the fourth divisions), and blending the model in doesn't improve on the line anywhere — the fitted market weight is 0 in every tier, and a simulation betting every side whose chance × closing odds clears 1 finds a few dozen bets a season, all noise. So where there is a price, the engine trusts it, prices every other market consistently from it (its favourites are calibrated to within a point in every bucket), and the honest edge is elsewhere: picks made before the line moves (measured by the closing line value), the games nobody prices where the model stands alone, and discipline — strong, calibrated legs only in the coupons. "Safe picks" at 72 %+ taken at the closing price landed 79.3 % of the time in the hold-out (predicted 79.2 %) for a −2.5 % return: calibrated, and about the bookmaker's margin — which is exactly what betting the close with no timing edge looks like, and why the closing line value on the record page is the number to watch.

### Hockey

Hockey has its own engine (`lib/hockey.js`, maths in `lib/hockey-core.js`): regulation goals as a Poisson pair with a fitted tie inflation, then overtime (3-on-3, decided by a goal about two thirds of the time) and the shootout. Expected goals come from a strength model — league scoring rate, home ice, goals-for/against and shot volumes (ESPN team statistics), last-ten form, the hockey Elo gap and rest days (back-to-backs) — whose weights `scripts/train-hockey.mjs` fits on five NHL seasons walk-forward (`npm run train:hockey`, report in `scripts/hockey-report.md`, parameters in `lib/hockey-params.json`). Where DraftKings prices the game, expected goals are solved from the de-vigged moneyline, total and puck line and the strength model nudges them; college and tournament games run on the strength model alone. Markets: moneyline (incl. OT & SO), 60-minute 1X2 and double chance, puck line ±1.5, totals 4.5–7.5 (OT goal counts, shootout goal doesn't), team totals, overtime yes/no, winning margins, exact regulation scores. Settlement knows how each game ended (ESPN's `Final/OT`, `Final/SO`).

ESPN carries only the NHL, NCAA hockey, the Olympics and the World Cup of Hockey. The European leagues come from **The Odds API** instead (`lib/oddsapi-leagues.js`, same `ODDS_API_KEY`): SHL, HockeyAllsvenskan, Liiga, Mestis and the AHL today, plus KHL / DEL / Swiss National League / Czech Extraliga the day the feed lists them (`lib/leagues.js`, entries with `source: "oddsapi"`). Fixtures and prices come from `/odds` (moneyline incl. OT & SO from the first 2-way book in `ODDS_BOOKMAKERS` order — on this feed 1xBet and the French books price the 60 minutes with a draw, Pinnacle / 888sport / Betsson the full game; main total from the first book listing it; `ODDS_HOCKEY_MARKETS=h2h,totals,spreads` adds the puck line for a third credit — untested whether the `eu` books return it), results from `/scores` (three days back). Without a puck line most picks on these leagues are totals at an estimated price (shown with ≈), which keeps them out of the top picks and the coupons; the NHL gets priced picks from DraftKings' puck line. Those games get the same hockey engine — no table there, so expected goals are solved from the prices and nudged by the league's scoring rate, the Elo ratings (learned on stable `oa:<league>:<team>` ids) and rest days — and the same saving/grading in the database. The results feed gives final scores only (no OT / shootout flag), so 60-minute and overtime markets are shown for information and never picked, and a shootout goal counts in the total. Credits: `/sports` is free and decides which leagues are in season; each league in season costs one credit per market (`h2h,totals` → 2) every `ODDS_HOCKEY_MIN` minutes (default `ODDS_CACHE_MIN` = 30) while the site is being visited, and 2 credits for results every `ODDS_SCORES_MIN` minutes (default 15) while a started game awaits its final score, else every `ODDS_SWEEP_MIN` (default 360, so finished games still feed the ratings). In October five leagues are in season, so the defaults spend up to ~500 credits a day — a paid plan (20 000/month) keeps them comfortably. The free plan (500/month ≈ 16 a day) fits one league refreshed four times a day: `ODDS_HOCKEY_LEAGUES=shl`, `ODDS_HOCKEY_MIN=360`, `ODDS_SCORES_MIN=60`, `ODDS_SWEEP_MIN=720`. Whatever the plan, the last `ODDS_RESERVE` credits (default 100) are never spent on these leagues, so the bookmaker comparison panel keeps working; the leagues then show their last fetched prices until the month resets.

### NFL

The NFL has its own engine (`lib/nfl.js`, maths in `lib/nfl-core.js`, parameters in `lib/nfl-params.json`, report in `scripts/nfl-report.md`). `npm run train:nfl` downloads every game since 2015 with ESPN's kept closing lines and both box scores (`scripts/nfl-history.mjs`, resumable, into `.cache/nfl`) and fits, walk-forward:

1. **Score grid** — the 60-minute score is a grid over (home, away) points: normal densities around the game's margin and total, times fitted weights per team score (3, 7, 10, 13, 14, 17, 20, 21, 24… common; 1 impossible) and per margin (the key numbers 3, 7, 10, 14). Fitted by maximum likelihood of every regulation score given the closing spread and total (team scores of 50+ and margins of 30+ share one pooled weight each). On the held-out 2025 season it gave a 3-point final margin 14.9% (actual 16.1% of 285 games; a plain normal curve says 7.4%) and improved the exact-score log-likelihood from −7.19 to −6.79 per game. Pushes on whole-number spreads matched in the fitted seasons (6.8% predicted, 4 of 58 games); the hold-out had none in 24 such games (1.7 expected), too few to say more.
2. **Overtime** — share of regular-season overtimes that end tied, a tilt of the winner toward the stronger side, and the points overtime adds (field goal, touchdown, both teams possessing).
3. **Elo** (538 style: K ≈ 20, home field, margin-of-victory multiplier, a share regressed to the mean each season) — `lib/ratings.js` starts every team from the ratings the trainer ended on.
4. **Strength model** — home field, Elo gap, points and yards-per-play differentials (turnovers and rest are tested and dropped when they don't help out of sample), and a total model.

The closing line is the consensus of real sportsbooks (prediction sites, aggregates and one provider whose stored lines are in-game ones left out; a book more than 3 points off the others dropped), solved with its own juice. On the held-out 2025 season it beats the strength model (margin error 12.19 vs 12.83 points; moneyline log loss 0.610 vs 0.633) and disagreeing with it didn't win against the spread (35–47% at 2–4 point gaps), so the fitted blend weight is ~0: the engine solves the game's margin and total from DraftKings' de-vigged moneyline, spread and total, then prices every market from the grid — moneyline (a tie voids), 60-minute 3-way, the book's spread ± 1/3/7 and the key-number lines, totals ± 3.5/7, team totals, winning-margin bands, overtime yes/no, odd/even. Skii's own line is shown next to the market's on the match page; without prices it prices the game alone. Overtime points come from today's rules (both teams possess: playoffs since 2022, regular season since 2025), shrunk toward the 2017+ table. Live: drive by drive — each side's remaining share of ~11 possessions scores field goals, touchdowns (with or without the extra point, or two) or safeties at the rate that matches the pre-game margin and total scaled by the time left, so the score never moves by 1 and nothing happens at 0:00; a calibrated tie tendency starts the live model exactly at the pre-game overtime chance, and overtime points count in the live totals. Preseason games are neither priced nor rated. The strength model's defence uses last season's yards allowed (ESPN's season statistics have no yards allowed; the trainer fits the same thing), and offense uses net yards (sacks taken off). Settlement handles ties and ESPN's `Final/OT`, `Final/2OT`. Re-run `npm run train:nfl` once a year (before week 1): the parameters carry priors for the season under way and the next one, and fresh Elo seeds.

Team ratings are stored per league for every sport except soccer (`nhl:1`, `nfl:1`): ESPN reuses team ids across sports, so before this the NHL's Bruins and the NFL's Falcons shared one rating. NHL and NFL teams start from the Elo their trainers ended on (`elo.seed` in the params files); games after the seed's date are applied on top (tracked in `skii_rated` as `k2:<event id>` outside soccer, so games the old code rated under shared ids are rated again under the new keys).

## Daily coupons, express builds, live grosse cote

- **Coupon du jour** — football first: the combination of at least two real-priced football legs (basketball, the NFL or baseball only when no strong football combination exists; never hockey) with the highest chance of landing at ~2.00 (x1.86 counts) (`lib/coupon.js` buildTarget, `lib/daily.js`). Every leg is one the model rates **Med-High or High** (72 %+ with bookmaker-anchored data). It always publishes: the next 30 h, then estimated prices are tried before Medium legs are allowed. No coupon is certain: at real prices a x2.00 slip lands about half the time; the rules only make sure no leg is a coin flip.
- **Leg rules for every coupon** (`PROFILES` in `lib/coupon.js`): no leg the model grades Low or Skip (x12 / x100 / x200 / x400 need Medium+, so a long-odds slip gets its odds from more strong legs, not underdogs); no game read without bookmaker prices (quality `stats` / `blind`, e.g. a youth World Cup); a leg can't contradict the game's own pick (score-grid check for football, hockey, NFL); and every coupon of the day backs the same market of a game. When the day can't reach a target under these rules, the closest build is published and the page says so.
- **Express builds, same day** — x12.5, x100 (the grosse cote du jour), x200 and x300/400, each the highest-chance combination of the day's priced legs; stored and graded like the coupon du jour (`skii_daily` kinds `x12`, `grosse`, `x200`, `x400`).
- **Live grosse cote** — `/api/live-coupon?target=20`, built from the games in play with the live models; prices are estimates (no free live odds feed).

The phone layout follows the sketch: four tabs (Accueil, Pronos, Stats, VIP), a header menu with every other page, snow (switchable) and the Juice WRLD music player (Spotify's official embed, switchable — songs can't be hosted). The home page reads only `/api/home`; the full board loads on the pages that need it.

## Special nights (Champions League…)

On a matchday of a big competition (`SPECIAL` in `lib/special.js`: Champions League with 3+ games today, Europa League / Conference League with 4+, World Cup, Euro, Copa América, CAN, Club World Cup, Libertadores) the site publishes a **special night** (`/special`, card on the home page, `/api/special`): every game of the slate with its real prices, the trained model's view, the line's movement since it opened and the engine's pick plus playable alternatives; one AI pass with web search over the whole slate (line-ups, injuries, suspensions, rotation, what is at stake) gives each game a verdict — banker / pick / avoid — and a one-line note; then the night's two coupons are built from the slate's priced legs with the games the AI flagged left out: **the night's coupon** (~x2, `special`) and **the night's grosse cote** (~x10, `special_x`, at most 2 estimated legs). Both are stored in `skii_daily` next to the daily coupons, graded with them and counted on the record page; the dossier is kept for the day in `skii_config` (`special:<day>`), so what was published before kick-off stays up. No AI key → published unreviewed, as the daily coupons are.

## Alerts, bots and the daily posts

- **In the browser**: toasts + optional pop-ups while the site is open; **push** alerts with the site closed (Settings → Alerts).
- **Kick-off alerts** (off until asked for; one button on the Live page, or Settings → Alerts → *Débuts de match*): one notification each time matches kick off — "⚽ 6 matchs commencent", the followed games first, then the day's top picks, then the bigger leagues — for every sport or football only. It is read off the schedule, not off the live feed (`lib/kick.js`): the page sends it while a tab is open, the tick sends it by push with the site closed, and both use the same tag so a device shows it once. A kick-off more than ten minutes old is never announced late.
- **Telegram**: create a bot with @BotFather, set `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `TELEGRAM_BOT_USERNAME`; make the bot an admin of your channel and set `TELEGRAM_CHANNEL` (e.g. `@skiipronos`). The webhook registers itself on the next daily run (or open `/api/telegram?key=<CRON_SECRET>`). Commands: `/start`, `/coupon`, `/grosse`, `/results`, `/alerts`, `/lang en`, `/link CODE`, `/stop`.
- **WhatsApp**: Meta Cloud API (developers.facebook.com → WhatsApp). Set `WHATSAPP_TOKEN`, `WHATSAPP_PHONE_ID`, `WHATSAPP_NUMBER`, `WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN`, point the webhook at `/api/whatsapp`, and (for messages sent outside the 24-hour window) an approved template name in `WHATSAPP_TEMPLATE`. Users write `start`, `coupon`, `grosse`, `résultats`, `lien CODE`, `stop`.
- **Linking an account**: Settings → Telegram / WhatsApp gives a one-time code; after `/start CODE` the chat gets goal alerts for the matches that account follows and results for its tracked coupons.
- **Daily posts**: coupon du jour + grosse cote at `POST_HOUR` (default 08:00 `SITE_TZ`) to the channel, subscribers and push devices; results at `RESULTS_HOUR` (23:00).
- **The tick** (`/api/tick?key=…`): the alert engine. It has to be called every minute: the production site installs that caller itself in the Supabase database (`pg_cron` + `pg_net`), once, from its daily job (`ensureScheduler` in `lib/alerts.js`; `/api/cron?when=scheduler` does it at once; `TICK_SCHEDULER=off` prevents it; `supabase/tick.sql` is the same thing by hand, with how to stop it); the two Vercel crons in `vercel.json` run it at least twice a day, which is enough for the daily posts but not for kick-off or goal alerts with the site closed. A call is cheap by design: it reads the saved schedule (a few small queries, no feed) to send the kick-off wave, and reads the live feed only while a followed game is being played. Once per quarter of an hour it runs the full pass (board, picks, tracked coupons, value spots, odds moves, daily posts) and saves the schedule for the next calls. `/api/tick?health` says whether it is running. The key is `TICK_SECRET`, `CRON_SECRET`, or the secret the app generates in `skii_config` (`tick_secret`).

## Settings (all optional)

See `.env.example`. Fixtures, scores, stats, form, standings and the main odds come from ESPN's public feed and need no key. Accounts, history, ratings, alerts and bots need the Supabase database (Vercel → Storage → Supabase); the app creates its tables itself.

**AI keys with a backup.** `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is the main key; `ANTHROPIC_API_KEYS` (comma-separated) lists backups, e.g. a key from a second account or billing. When a key is out of credits (the API answers 402, or 400 "credit balance is too low"), invalid, forbidden or rate-limited, the next key takes over on the spot and the spent key rests (6 hours, 2 minutes for a rate limit), so the AI analyst and the coupon check keep working until every key is exhausted. `ENGINE_MODEL_FALLBACK` names a second model tried when the engine model is unavailable or overloaded.

**Scoring markets.** A Poisson score matrix fitted to a bookmaker's 1X2 and total predicts too many blanks: on 36,370 closing lines a side scored at least once 1.3 points more often than it said, both sides scored 1.7 points more often and 0-0 came 1 point less often, in the training seasons and in the hold-out one alike. `lib/model.js` shifts those markets (team to score, clean sheets, both teams to score and its combinations, over/under 0.5) by constants fitted on those games; every other market family was within a point and is left alone, and a sharper favourite/long-shot correction or another score-matrix shape (other Dixon-Coles ρ, bivariate Poisson, negative binomial) did not beat the current one on the hold-out season.

**Live.** A game in play is re-priced from its pre-match expected goals, the score, red cards and the share of goals still to come at that minute (`lib/model.js liveAnalyse`: measured on 1,290 goals, 44.5% come before half-time and one in fifteen in second-half stoppage time, so a straight minutes-left clock under-counts late goals by a fifth). How the score changes each side's scoring was measured on 1,837 bookmaker-priced games replayed minute by minute (`scripts/backtest/live*.mjs`): a side one goal up scores at its pre-match rate (the engine used to assume 10% less), two or more up 10% more, one down 9% more, two or more down 15% more. With these, every live market at 55–85% reads 70.0% and lands 69.9% on the half of the games kept aside. A fresh server instance rebuilds the bookmaker's pre-match read from the chances saved before kick-off.

**The OP live pick.** One single bet, not a coupon: among the football games in play that a bookmaker priced, between the 6th and the 80th minute, the likeliest bet whose estimated price is still worth taking (≈1.25+, ≈1.50+ or ≈2.00+), never the plain draw (the one bet the replay found over-rated). Every live price is the engine's chance less the same margin, so no live bet is better value than another: what the engine ranks is the chance, and the test checks that this chance is true. Replayed every five minutes of five weeks from the games' real kick-off times, the 3,490 picks of the ≈1.25+ level carried 73.4% and won 73.5% (≈1.50+: 60.5% → 60.2%; ≈2.00+: 45.1% → 45.8%). At the estimated price they lose the bookmaker's margin like any bet; the card shows the fair price next to it.

**The live coupon holds its bets.** The live coupon ("Coupon live", Live and VIP pages) used to be rebuilt from nothing every 30 seconds, each leg being the bet priced closest to what the target needed. With one game in play that was whichever bet stood nearest 50%, and it jumped to the opposite bet as the clock ran: replayed over 2,108 games (`scripts/backtest/livehold.mjs`), the "Safe" coupon on a single game changed 9.3 times a game with nothing happening on the pitch, 3.5 of them to the exact opposite bet (BTTS yes → no, over → under). Now every leg is a likely one (62% or more on a x2–x3 slip, 55% above), so the coupon stays short of its target rather than take a coin flip, and the page sends back the legs it shows (`?keep`): a leg stays while it is still open, its chance no more than 7 points under the floor and its price no lower than four fifths of what it was; only a leg that fails is replaced, and a line under the coupon says which and why. Same replay: 1.9 quiet changes a game on one game, none to the opposite bet (4.3 on two games, against 18.5), legs announced at 67.7% and landing 67.2%.

**The OP cote 2.** The same card's fourth choice, shown first when no football is in play: a coupon at odds 2 from the next games (6 or 24 hours), built the way a careful bettor builds one by hand (`lib/opcote.js`). Football games a bookmaker prices, a side to win at its real price (1.18–1.60), never credited with more chance than that price implies; of every pair and treble whose odds reach 2, the one with the highest chance. Rebuilt for each of 622 past days from closing prices (`scripts/backtest/opcote.mjs`), it was announced at 46.5% and landed 49.0% (±2.0), for −0.6% per unit staked. Over/under legs carry more margin and did not help; three short favourites (≤1.35) are no safer than the best pair (45.6% announced, 46.9% landed). `/live` and `/vip` offer the best live coupon for a target — the fewest legs that reach it, since every extra leg pays the margin again — and a builder for the visitor's own, one market per game; every live price is an estimate (≈).

**Corners, cards and shots on target.** `lib/extras.js` prices total corners, team corners, most corners, total yellow cards and shots on target (total, per team, most; shots faced = saves + goals conceded) for football in the bigger competitions (tier 1–2) from each team's season figures on ESPN's core API (a count model, slightly wider than Poisson, early-season figures pulled toward the average). No free feed prices these markets, so every one is an estimate (≈); they are offered on the match page and to the big-odds builds, never as the engine's own pick. A leg is graded from the match summary's final corners, cards and shots on target (voided a day after kick-off if ESPN kept none). The model has not been back-tested yet.

**Token saving.** The numeric engine prices every match for free; the AI is kept for the ones that matter (`lib/ai-gate.js`). The analyst button exists only on the big competitions (Champions / Europa League, the top-five leagues, World Cup, Euros, Club World Cup, and NBA / NFL / NHL playoff games) and never on a finished match; other matches show a note instead, and the server refuses them too. One analysis is cached and shared by every visitor (3 hours before kick-off, 5 minutes live), a daily cap (`AI_DAILY_MAX`, default 30) applies across all server instances, the coupon du jour check only reads legs from those competitions (and skips the grosse cote), and the Champions League night review is limited to a few web searches. `AI_LEAGUES` changes the list.

**Speed.** The site is static HTML on Vercel's edge; the data comes from small JSON routes (`/api/home` a few KB, `/api/board` ~50 KB compressed) cached 15–20 s on the CDN. Building the board from ESPN takes 10–20 s on a cold server instance (20 MB of feeds that don't fit Next's fetch cache), so the client board and the home payload are also kept as snapshots in the database (`skii_cache`): a fresh instance answers from the last snapshot at once (up to 15 minutes old) and rebuilds on the next poll. The browser bundle stays light: the prediction engine is server-only (the browser imports `lib/settle.js` and `lib/coupon-lite.js`), and only the pages visitors open next are prefetched.
